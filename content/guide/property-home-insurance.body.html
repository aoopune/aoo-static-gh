What does it pay for?

Repair or rebuild after covered damage.

Take cover at possession of a finished house. Until handover, risk for the unfinished house usually sits with the seller or builder.

  • Building and contents Building is the structure. Land is not insured. Contents are the things inside, and only if you add contents cover.

Often covered

Fire, lightning, earthquake, storm, flood, and other events listed on the policy. Theft or burglary can vary by policy.

Usually not covered

Intentional damage, war, nuclear risks, and losses the policy does not cover. A drop in market value is usually not covered.

How is the cover set up?

The written details sit on your Policy Schedule.

  1. House address The address on the policy must match the house.
  2. Cover amount Check the cover amount before you sign. It should match rebuild cost of the structure, not market price of flat plus land. Too low and the lender may reject the policy, and a claim can be scaled down by the same shortfall. Insuring for less than rebuild cost is under-insurance.
  3. Cover period Many home policies last about 10 years. Longer brand packages exist. They help only if amount, perils, and lender naming still match your agreement. If your loan is longer, renew on time and keep the bank named on each renewal.
  4. Rebuild or loan-only Some policies pay to rebuild the home. Others only clear what you still owe the bank. Outstanding-loan packages and full rebuild policies are different products. Ask which one you are buying.

How big is the premium?

Usually smaller than loan cover.

  • When you pay Most policies renew yearly — you pay the insurer and keep the receipt. Some packs cover several years in one premium, often about 10. If your home loan runs longer, plan the next renewal before cover ends.
  • Premium inside the home loan Less common than for loan cover, but some offers still club a property premium into the loan. If that happens, you pay interest on that premium too.
  • Missed renewal A lapsed policy leaves the house uninsured. The bank may also flag the loan if the agreement required continuous cover with the lender named.
  • GST on the premium Property cover is general insurance and currently carries 18% GST. It shows on your premium receipt.

What after damage?

Follow the claim steps.

  1. First steps Make the property safe. Tell the insurer soon — many policies want notice within about 30 days. Keep damaged items for survey unless you must move them for safety.
  2. What to keep Policy number, photos, and any police or fire report if they apply. Do not discard debris or start non-urgent repairs before the survey unless the insurer agrees.
How payout works

Partial damage usually pays repair cost. Total loss pays up to the cover amount on your schedule. Rebuild policies aim to restore the home; loan-only packages may pay the bank up to what you still owe.2

If the bank is named

The bank is usually on the building policy as loss payee or under an Agreed Bank Clause. How money flows depends on your policy and that clause — especially on a total loss. On partial damage, many rebuild policies reimburse repair cost to you, but the bank may still need to be told and may need to approve release of funds.

Building vs contents

Contents claims are often separate from the building. Contents payout may go to you even when the bank is named on the building cover.

On total loss, the bank is usually named on the building payout. Contents can still pay out separately.
