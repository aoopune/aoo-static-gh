01

Coverage

Coverage

Pays toward the outstanding home loan on death of the Life Assured. Covers the main borrower — and a co-borrower if named on the Certificate of Insurance.

Often covered

Death of the Life Assured. Serious illness, permanent disability, or accidental death when named on the Certificate of Insurance.

Usually not covered

Anything not named on the Certificate of Insurance. Illness before the waiting period ends. Claims in the first year often pay less than the full amount.

Already have term insurance? The bank may accept assignment of that cover to the loan instead of new loan cover.

02

Setup

Cover setup

The Certificate of Insurance holds these details.

  1. When cover starts Usually when the loan is disbursed and the insurer has received payment.
  2. The loan The home loan on the Certificate of Insurance must match the loan account. A mismatch means no cover.
  3. Reducing or level Reducing goes down as the loan is repaid. Level stays the same, and costs more. While the house is being built, cover often stays flat until EMIs begin.
  4. How long cover runs Should match the loan term. Some plans renew every year instead.2
03

Premium

Premium

These premiums are usually larger.

When you pay

Most often once at the start, paid outside the loan. Some plans take yearly or monthly payments instead.

Premium inside the home loan

The premium is added to the loan principal and repaid through instalments. Interest applies on that premium for the remaining tenure, so the same cover can cost more this way. Needs clear consent. Buying stays voluntary.

Use term cover bought separately

Premium is paid to the insurer directly. The bank may ask to be named on the policy. That is assignment. It is not the same as bank-bundled loan cover.

Free-look window

Many life policies allow a short cancel period after issue. Confirm the days, the refund path, and where the money returns if the premium sat inside the loan.

Tax on the premium

Life cover premiums may qualify under Section 80C in the old tax regime, within the overall limit. When the premium is borrowed and repaid through instalments, that path often does not qualify. Keep the receipt.

GST on the premium

Individual life cover premiums are currently exempt from GST↗ (opens official CBIC GST notification). Confirm what shows on your premium receipt.

04

Claim

Claim

How the payout works, and who is on it.

  • What gets paid out Many plans pay the amount on the Certificate of Insurance, not today's exact loan balance.1
  • How to claim The nominee or the bank submits the papers. If approved, the loan is reduced or cleared.
  • If the loan is prepaid or transferred If you close the loan early or move it to another bank, cover does not always end. This cover often cannot come with you, and you may get only some of the premium back — some plans return nothing. Ask for the refund and cover status in writing.3
05

Compare

Term plan or loan cover

Two ways to clear the loan on death of the Life Assured. They price and behave differently.

Term plan

  • Cost Usually less per rupee of cover. Paid outside the loan.
  • Cover Stays level. Can outlast the loan.
  • Early repay or switch Stays with you.
  • Who gets paid Your nominee. They can clear the loan and keep the rest.

Loan cover

  • Cost Often a single premium. Costs more when it sits inside the loan.
  • Cover Usually reduces as the loan is repaid.
  • Early repay or switch Often cannot move. May return only part of the premium.
  • Who gets paid The bank first, up to the outstanding loan.

If age or health makes a term plan hard to get, loan cover can be easier to buy, and can be paid through the loan.

Property cover and loan cover are separate products. One does not replace the other. Both covers overview · Property cover
