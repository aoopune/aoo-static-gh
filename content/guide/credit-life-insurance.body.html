What does it pay for?

The outstanding home loan if the borrower dies.

Often covered

Death of the borrower named on the cover. Serious illness, permanent disability, or accidental death when those events are named on your policy papers.

Usually not covered

Anything not named on your policy papers. Illness before the waiting period ends. Claims in the first year often pay less than the full amount.

Already have term insurance? The bank may accept assignment of that cover to the loan instead of new loan cover.

How is the cover set up?

The written details are found on your Certificate of Insurance.

  1. When cover starts Usually when the loan is disbursed and the insurer has received payment.
  2. The loan The home loan named on that certificate must match the loan account. A mismatch means no cover.
  3. How long cover runs Should match the loan term. A few plans renew every year instead. That is not the same as cover matched to the full loan.

Reducing goes down as the loan is repaid. Level stays the same, and costs more. While the house is being built, cover often stays flat until EMIs begin.

How big is the premium?

It is usually higher than property cover.

Most often once at the start, paid outside the loan. Some plans take yearly or monthly payments instead.

The premium is added to the loan principal and repaid through instalments. Interest applies on that premium for the remaining tenure, so the same cover can cost more this way. Needs clear consent. Buying stays voluntary.

Premium is paid to the insurer directly. The bank may ask to be named on the policy. That is assignment. It is not the same as bank-bundled loan cover.

Many life policies allow a short cancel period after issue. Confirm the days, the refund path, and where the money returns if the premium sat inside the loan.

Tax

Life cover premiums may qualify under Section 80C in the old tax regime, within the overall limit. When the premium is borrowed and repaid through instalments, that path often does not qualify. Keep the receipt.

GST

Individual life cover premiums are currently exempt from GST↗ (opens official CBIC GST notification). Confirm what shows on your premium receipt.

How does a claim work?

The payout clears or reduces the loan.

Many plans pay the amount written on the certificate, not today's exact loan balance. Some plans track the live loan balance only if that certificate says so.

The nominee or the bank submits the papers. If approved, the loan is reduced or cleared.

If you close the loan early or move it to another bank, cover does not always end. This cover often cannot come with you, and you may get only some of the premium back — some plans return nothing. Any refund follows the surrender terms on your certificate. Ask for the refund and cover status in writing.

Term plan or loan cover?

These are two ways to clear the loan upon death.

Term plan

  • Cost Usually less per rupee of cover. Paid outside the loan.
  • Cover Stays level. Can outlast the loan.
  • Early repay or switch Stays with you.
  • Who gets paid Your nominee. They can clear the loan and keep the rest.

Loan cover

  • Cost Often a single premium. Costs more when it sits inside the loan.
  • Cover Usually reduces as the loan is repaid.
  • Early repay or switch Often cannot move. May return only part of the premium.
  • Who gets paid The bank first, up to the outstanding loan.

If age or health makes a term plan hard to get, loan cover can be easier to buy, and can be paid through the loan.

Education only. Your Certificate of Insurance wins if anything here differs.
