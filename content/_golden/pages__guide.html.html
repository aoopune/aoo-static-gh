How much can you borrow?

Max loan amount is the lower of your property value limit or income capacity.

1. Property value limit

Limited to the percentages of the Agreement Value listed below (as per RBI limits):

  • Up to ₹30 lakh: up to 90%.
  • From ₹30 lakh to ₹75 lakh: up to 80%.
  • Above ₹75 lakh: up to 75%.

Amount can be more or less depending on your profile and lender rules.

2. Income capacity

Limited to what your monthly budget can support, calculated as follows:

  • Start with your monthly take-home income.
  • Subtract your current EMIs and a portion of your credit card limits (total EMIs can generally use up 55% to 70% of your income).
  • Final loan amount is then calculated using this leftover amount, interest rate, and tenure.

Adding an earning co-applicant in the loan increases the loan amount.

How do you repay the loan?

Through an Equated Monthly Instalment (EMI) — includes both the principal and the interest.

  • In the early years, you pay mostly interest because you owe the most money, while later, you pay mostly toward the principal.
  • EMI stays fixed on a fixed rate, but moves up or down on a floating rate (if rates change).
  • For ready-to-move properties, full EMI payments begin roughly one month after the initial disbursement.
  • For under-construction properties, you pay interest only on the disbursed amount until full EMIs kick in near possession.

Some lenders let you pay more than interest during construction. Ask if that option exists.

Up to what period can you repay?

Maximum tenure is typically 30 years, subject to age limits and lender guidelines. Extending the tenure reduces the EMI but drives up overall interest costs.

What are the types of interest rates?

Floating rates, which change over time, and fixed rates, which stay the same for a few years.

The interest rate is linked to an external benchmark, such as the RBI repo rate, plus the bank's spread.

  • The RBI reviews the repo rate every two months, and whenever it changes, banks must adjust your interest rate.
  • If rates rise, you can manage costs by paying a lump sum, raising your EMI, extending your tenure, or combining these options
  • PerRBI guidelines↗(opens official RBI page), banks cannot charge prepayment or foreclosure penalties on floating-rate home loans.

The interest rate stays fixed for an initial period and then switches to a floating rate.

  • An additional fixed-rate premium is added to the benchmark (external or internal) and the bank's margin.
  • This fixed period usually lasts for 3 to 5 years, depending on your lender.
  • During this fixed period, your rate is typically 1% to 2% higher than floating rates.
  • Prepaying or closing your loan incurs a 2% to 4% penalty on the amount paid.

Rate options after sanction

Options you have after taking a loan:

1. Switch between fixed and floating rates.

2. Move to a different benchmark.

3. Ask for a rate cut if credit scores improve.

For existing loans, floating rates automatically adjust within three months when the RBI changes the repo rate.

For new loans, if RBI recently changed the rate, it may take up to 90 days to update.

Note: All changes are subject to bank approval and applicable fees.

What are the types of loan facilities?

A regular term loan or an overdraft (where spare cash reduces interest).

Interest is charged only on your remaining loan balance as you pay it off.

  • EMI and loan tenure stay fixed unless you make a formal prepayment.
  • Ideal if you don't leave extra money in the bank for a long time.

Extra money in your account lowers your interest, effectively acting as savings while remaining withdrawable anytime.

  • EMI stays the same, but more goes toward the principal—paying off your loan faster.
  • The interest rate is 0.15% to 1% higher than on a term loan.
  • This saves money only if the interest you cut with extra cash is higher than the loan's extra rate.
  • Lenders may charge non-utilisation fees if you use too little of your sanctioned limit.

Rules after you choose a facility

These rules matter when you choose.

You can switch between term loan and overdraft later, if the bank allows it and you pay their switch fee.

On overdraft, withdrawing parked money follows the bank’s limit and rules.

On overdraft, the usable limit may fall over the years (dropline), as per the lender.

What are the loan charges?

Bank fees, property check charges, and government charges.

Bank fees

Charges to open and manage your loan.

  • Processing fee(Non-refundable) A one-time fee to process and approve your loan. Part may be taken upfront as a login fee; the rest at final sanction.
  • Others Account handling fees or any other as per lender.

Other fees

Fees to check the property and register the loan and security.

  • Legal and technical fees To check the property and its legal papers.
  • Title search report fees To check that the property title is clear and free from encumbrances.
  • Valuation fees To check the property's market value.
  • Government charges To register the loan & security.

Notes

  • Insurance and other add-ons are separate from the processing fee.
  • Checks are conducted exclusively by the bank, and outside reports provided by you will not be accepted.
  • As perRBI guidelines Chapter VIII B. (5)↗(opens official RBI page), only charges listed in the Key Fact Statement (KFS) are enforceable, so read every line before paying upfront.

Is your project bank-approved?

Banks verify and approve a property before releasing your loan, which is known as Approved Project Finance (APF).

For new or under-construction projects, banks approve the entire property upfront, saving buyers time and legal fees — though not all banks approve every project.

For resale properties, an individual APF is required for each unit, causing extra legal fees and longer approval times.
