has tax benefits.

Can you claim the interest on tax return?

Yes, your limit depends on who lives in the home and on the tax regime. Section 22↗ (opens official Income Tax page) ITA 2025

What changes this limit

  • ₹2 lakh limit Needs a loan to buy or build, and the home ready within 5 years. If either is missing, only ₹30,000 a year.
  • Loan taken for repair, renewal or reconstruction Only ₹30,000 a year.

Old tax regime

No fixed upper cap

 

New tax regime

Can be claimed against rental income

 

The 5-year ready rule does not apply when you rent it out.

Up to two homes can be treated as self-occupied.

Can you claim the principal on tax return?

Yes, only under the old tax regime. Schedule XV↗ (opens official Income Tax page) ITA 2025

Up to

₹1.5 lakh

a year under the old tax regime

  • The principal repayment limit under Section 80C is shared with other eligible investments, such as EPF contributions, life insurance premiums, and PPF.
  • You can only claim tax benefits on principal repayment starting from the financial year in which construction is completed and you take possession of the property.

If you sell the home within 5 years of getting possession, all previously claimed Section 80C principal deductions are added back to your taxable income for that year; however, Section 24(b) interest deductions are not reversed.

What if the home is under construction?

Claim the interest starting from the year you get the keys.

  1. Pre-construction interest accrued prior to taking possession can be claimed in five equal annual installments starting from the year of possession.
  2. Post-possession interest paid during the year can be claimed in full on an annual basis under Section 24(b).

About the ₹2 lakh interest limit

Under the old tax regime, pre-possession and after-possession interest together stay inside the ₹2 lakh limit.

The ₹2 lakh limit does not apply when you rent it out.

Can both co-owners claim tax benefits?

Yes, when both names are on the property papers and the loan, and each pays their share of the EMI. Each can claim within their ownership share and what they actually paid.

How do you claim it?

Interest and principal go in different sections of your tax return.

If salaried, share the interest and principal figures with your employer for TDS.

Documents you need

Interest certificate

From the lender for the year. Shows interest payment and principal repayment split.

Possession proof

Needed when principal repayment and pre-possession interest payment start.

Anything else on tax?

There are a few extra benefits beyond the main interest and principal deductions.

Stamp duty and registration (property purchase only)

Claimed in the year you pay them, within the same ₹1.5 lakh principal repayment basket. Not loan costs.

Home loan protection insurance

Premium can sit in the same ₹1.5 lakh basket if you pay it yourself — not if the lender adds it to your EMI.

House property loss (old tax regime)

If interest creates a loss, only up to ₹2 lakh can be set off against other income like salary in that year. The rest can be carried forward.

House property loss (new tax regime)

Interest on a rented home can still reduce rental income. A loss from this usually cannot cut other income like salary.

Loan for a plot only (no house yet)

You usually claim principal repayment on tax only after a house is built on that plot and you have possession.

Older first-home extras (only if your loan year matches)

Under the old tax regime only, and only if this was your first home, older rules allowed extra interest for loans sanctioned in fixed windows. Section 130↗ (opens official Income Tax page) covered sanctions from 1 April 2016 to 31 March 2017 (up to ₹50,000 extra; loan up to ₹35 lakh; house up to ₹50 lakh). Section 131↗ (opens official Income Tax page) covered sanctions from 1 April 2019 to 31 March 2022 (up to ₹1.5 lakh extra; stamp value up to ₹45 lakh). This sits over and above the Section 22 interest limit. You cannot claim both. Not for loans sanctioned after 31 March 2022.

For general understanding only. Home loan tax rules depend on your facts, the tax regime you choose, and current law. Confirm claims with a qualified tax professional before you file.
