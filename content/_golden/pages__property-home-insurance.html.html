What does it pay for?

Insure your property for its physical reconstruction cost, excluding the land and market price.

  1. Coverage timing Take out coverage only upon taking possession of a finished house, as the builder bears the risk until handover.
  2. What is Included The physical structure is covered, while contents are included only if you add extra protection.
  3. Events Covered Fire, lightning, earthquake, storm, flood, and listed events, with theft or burglary varying by policy.
  4. Events Excluded Intentional damage, war, nuclear risks, market value drops, and unlisted perils.

How is the cover set up?

The written details are found on your Policy Schedule.

  1. House Address The policy address must precisely match the house.
  2. Cover Amount Ensure the cover amount matches the rebuild cost of the structure, not the market price. Insuring for less risks a lender rejecting the policy or claims being proportionally scaled down.
  3. Cover Period Policies often last around 10 years. Renew on time to match your loan duration and keep the bank named on each renewal.
  1. Full Rebuild The payout covers the complete cost of rebuilding your home, no matter how much you still owe the bank.
  2. Loan-Only The payout only covers the exact amount left on your mortgage, leaving you with nothing if the rebuild costs more than your remaining loan.

What are the policy and payment terms?

Keep your policy continuously active to avoid loan violations, and pay either annually or bundled into your mortgage.

Pay annually upon yearly renewal, or cover multiple years upfront (such as a 10-year package), ensuring you renew before any long-term home loan term expires.

Some offers bundle the insurance premium directly into your mortgage, meaning you will pay loan interest on that amount over time.

A lapsed policy leaves your home uninsured and may trigger a loan violation if your lender requires continuous coverage.

Property insurance is subject to an 18% GST, which is clearly listed on your premium receipt.

What to do after damage?

Notify your insurer, preserve evidence, and follow your claim, payout, and bank procedures.

  1. Notify Promptly Make the property safe and inform your insurer within the required timeframe (often within 30 days).
  2. Preserve Evidence Keep your policy number, photos, reports, and damaged items or debris for the survey until the insurer approves cleanup or repairs.
  1. Partial Damage Covers the cost of repairs.
  2. Total Loss Pays up to the insured limit, either restoring the home (rebuild policy) or settling your remaining mortgage (loan-only policy).
  1. Loss Payee The bank is named on the building policy; on total loss it is paid first up to what you owe, then leftover comes to you — and it may also need to approve partial repair funds.
  2. Building vs. Contents While the bank controls the building payout during a total loss, contents insurance pays out separately.
