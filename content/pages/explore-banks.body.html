Add a co-applicant with income to borrow more.

  1. A co-applicant's income gets added to yours. Banks recalculate your EMI room from the combined number.
  2. A second salary of ₹50,000 can add roughly ₹30 to 40 lakh to the loan, depending on existing EMIs (extra income under FOIR).
  3. Their EMIs count too. Add them only if their income is clean and their loans are low.

    Borrower
    1. Tick this if you work for the government, a PSU, or draw a pension.
    2. Some banks hold a lower rate for these jobs — usually 0.05 to 0.25% off.
    3. On ₹50 lakh over 20 years that's around ₹78,000 saved (from that rate cut).
    4. Bank checks from your employment letter. Get it in the sanction letter, not just agreed at the branch.
    Concessions
    1. Tick what applies to you. Each cut is usually 0.05 to 0.10% off the rate; some stack.
    2. 0.20% on ₹50 lakh over 20 years saves about ₹1.5 lakh (from stacked cuts).
    3. Insurance is the one to watch: the rate cut can cost less than the premium they add to your loan.
    4. You can't be forced to buy their cover.

    Bank type
    1. Narrows the table to public banks, private banks, or both.
    2. Public usually runs 0.25 to 0.50% lower on rate but needs more branch visits.
    3. Private is faster, somewhat higher rate and fees.
    4. Leave both on to see the full market. You can move to another bank later.
    Rate
    1. Floating moves when RBI changes the repo rate. Fixed stays locked for a period, usually 5 years.
    2. Fixed is about 1 to 2% higher. On ₹50 lakh over 20 years that's ₹3,300 more a month (from that higher fixed rate).
    3. Floating home loans to individuals have no prepayment charge; fixed loans can.
    4. Know that before you pick fixed thinking it's safer.

    Facility
    1. Term loan is the same EMI every month.
    2. Overdraft links the loan to a savings account — money you keep there reduces the interest you pay.
    3. ₹20 lakh loan with ₹5 lakh in the linked account, you pay interest on ₹15 lakh (loan minus parked balance). You can take that ₹5 lakh out any time.
    4. Overdraft costs 0.15 to 1% more per year. Worth it only if you'll actually keep a good amount sitting in that account.
