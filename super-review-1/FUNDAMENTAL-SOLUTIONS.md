## 1. Balance the first thing people see

1 source file

The first block people meet should feel evenly balanced. Empty space above the words and below the main button should match so the pair sits in the middle of that block.

## 2. Say the same story in shorter, simpler lines

2 source files

Keep the story people already like, but say it in shorter lines with mostly one clear thought each. Reading should take less effort, and crude asks for personal details should be softened.

## 3. Supporting lines must add value or the stretch can go

1 source file

A strong heading still needs smaller lines that actually help. If those lines add little, rewrite them, skip that stretch, or redesign it.

## 4. Do not leave unfinished words that look broken

2 source files

When a big claim scrolls in, do not leave bare pieces that make people think the page is broken. Reveal the full claim in order, one beat after another, so the words arrive with the numbers.

## 5. One clear voice about fairness, without inviting how you earn

2 source files

Speak in one unmixed voice about standing fair and not pushing anyone. Avoid wording that sounds like a customer fee of zero, or that makes people ask how you make money.

## 6. No fake urgency — people go at their own pace

1 source file

Do not pile overlapping scare words about calls and hard sells. Say plainly that you bring everything, you do not create urgency, and people can look at their own pace without extra nags.

## 7. Fewer story blocks when the same strength repeats

3 source files

If several full-screen story blocks repeat the same strengths, fold them into fewer blocks. Keep what is unique; drop thin slides that only restate what is already said.

## 8. Keep the journey title and make the strongest point bigger

1 source file

Keep a clear journey title with a few sections under it. Make the first, unique strength much larger so people see the main gift before the rest.

## 9. Let people read every point without hunting clicks

1 source file

Do not force people through a one-line-at-a-time reveal to learn what you offer. Let the full set of points be readable without extra hunting.

## 10. Show the real thing you claim, not a cheap word duplicate

2 source files

If you claim a product view, show that real view in the main place people look. Do not park the same words again beside the claim like a cheap marketing trick.

## 11. Prefer plain words over fancy pictures that add friction

1 source file

When a claim can be said in words right away, say it in words. Do not add extra clicks or complex pictures that make the claim feel harder or less honest.

## 12. Help people toward what they need, while they still choose

1 source file

A real strength is optional help toward what someone needs. Say what that help means, and keep the final choice with the person.

## 13. One idea per section, not an essay

1 source file

Each section should carry one clear idea. Do not pile several big claims into one block as if you were writing an essay.

## 14. Reuse strong wording even if its old section goes

1 source file

If you drop a whole section, you can still salvage a strong phrase somewhere else if it still earns its place.

## 15. Help sits with the bottom support area, not as its own story

1 source file

A help strip that is useful should sit flush with the bottom of the page on every page that has one. Do not treat it as another full-screen story section between the main story blocks.

## 16. Important trust words should not live only in ignored bottom legal text

1 source file

People ignore the bottom legal area. If something matters for trust, show it where people actually read — not only down there.

## 17. Keep the legal limits, but sound like you stand with the customer

1 source file

You can still say you are not the lender and do not approve loans. Say it as someone on the customer’s side, not as someone washing their hands.

## 18. Write so a helpful assistant could work with the same words

1 source file

Write site words so a helpful assistant could listen and work with them the same way a person would. Treat that as a standing writing rule, not a one-block rewrite.

## 19. Sensible defaults ready so people can act without typing first

3 source files

Open with sensible numbers already filled so people can see options right away. A ready example profile also helps, because many people will not type their true current figures.

## 20. When more than one choice can be true, keep more than one on

1 source file

When more than one choice can be true at once, let people keep more than one on. Do not force them to turn the others off with an exclusive all-or-one control.

## 21. Keep gaps that make section titles feel like titles

1 source file

Space between filter groups can make each heading feel like a real section title. Keep those gaps when they already help people read the hierarchy.

## 22. Explain what each filter choice means where people decide

3 source files

Do not send people to another page to learn what a filter means. On the spot, explain trade-offs, discounts, and hard ideas with real examples so they know what they are picking.

## 23. Helper text should say why a field matters, in everyday words

2 source files

Beside a money field, answer why you ask and what the answer does. Do not open with vague words like “sets” or “ceiling” that people in India do not use.

## 24. Name the field so people know which figure to type

2 source files

Use a short label that names the figure people should enter. Teach when to use the sale-agreement amount versus a careful bank-style value, so they do not type felt market price by mistake.

## 25. Let people work in score bands, not only one exact number

3 source files

People often know a score window, not one exact digit. Let them choose or type in bands or ranges, with clear progressive suggestions, so results feel honest and usable.

## 26. Show today’s options and what becomes possible if the score rises

1 source file

Let people see what they get at the score they have now, and what opens if the score improves. Do not force them to type a hoped-for higher score that empties the whole list.

## 27. When a lender gives a score band, show both rates and both loans

1 source file

If the offer comes as a score window, show the matching rates and loan amounts for that window. One single rate hides which offer is lowest and on what basis.

## 28. Clear tips that save money, standing on the customer’s side

9 source files

People want clear tips that can save a lot of money, not only a list of today’s offers. Stand on their side like an advocate: say what to change, how much that saves, and how to get a better deal with small extra effort.

## 29. Show advice beside the search, without muddying the comparison list

2 source files

Put money-saving tips where people already searched, so they do not learn a second screen. Keep the plain comparison list clear — do not stuff the tips into the rows until the list gets muddy.

## 30. Default a middle score so people who think they are higher look again

1 source file

Keep one score field and start it at a middle default. Someone who believes they are higher will type their number and notice what they were missing.

## 31. Tell people plainly whether a price looks low, high, or unlikely to fall

3 source files

Tell people plainly whether a price or fee looks low, high, or unlikely to fall. Do not use fake sale urgency that makes a fool of them.

## 32. Show help as a normal product recommendation, not machine magic

1 source file

Show the help as a normal product recommendation. Do not label it as machine magic, because that can break trust.

## 33. Grow by satisfying the next few people, not a mass crowd

3 source files

Launch by truly satisfying the next customer, then the next few after that. Measure success in small steps, start with people who really need it, and climb toward something everyone can use — not a mass funnel for show.

## 34. Pay for real feedback and talk to early users deeply

1 source file

Survey customers, offer gift cards, and even pay when strong feedback is worth it. Talk with early users in a heavy loop, not only the happy path.

## 35. Honesty about who you are, with trust first

1 source file

People can appreciate honest “we are small and working hard” even if the site also looks clean for money people. Trust is non-negotiable either way.

## 36. Ship a guide and tools before each new release

1 source file

Treat every new feature as its own release. Have the guide and tools ready first, like an app update note, before moving on.

## 37. Keep the score as a free number people can type

1 source file

For the credit score, keep a free number field people can type. Do not force that field into a range picker meant for other controls.

## 38. Prove advice works by trying to game it for real

1 source file

The only way to learn if money-saving advice is true is to try gaming the form for real. Description alone is not enough.

## 39. Stay opinionated, and listen across many income levels

2 source files

Build from a clear product stance, not from every random request. Listen across many income levels so the first few paying customers do not become the whole product.

## 40. Form, tips, and comparison should make sense in one glance

1 source file

On first look, people should see the filled form, a few tip notes, and at least a couple of comparison rows together. Tips come from the filled form; the list below is for comparing.

## 41. When inputs change, show the rate first and where numbers come from

1 source file

Changing income, property, or age should cascade clearly. Show the rate first, and add short tips that explain where calculated numbers come from, so pieces do not get orphaned.

## 42. Questions that change the answer stay in view, already filled

6 source files

Questions that change the answer should stay in view and already filled in. People should see that filling them matters, and they should not get a surprise later when hidden values still move the result.

## 43. Put the action to see results next to where people just changed things

1 source file

After someone opens or changes extra questions, the button to see results should sit right there, centered near that work. They should not hunt a separate button on the side.

## 44. Do not coach people to game the form to inflate the loan

1 source file

Do not teach applicants that adding another person on the form is a trick to inflate the loan. Help honestly about who would actually pay — do not coach gaming.

## 45. Show how much each question matters

2 source files

Mark how much each question moves the answer — with stars, a simple meter, order, or color. Filling the form itself should teach what matters most without a separate lecture.

## 46. Name the main action for comparing, not vague exploring

2 source files

The main button and page job should say compare, because that is what people are doing. “Explore” or “see options” can sound like dumping everything in.

## 47. Arrange the help notes so they are easy to read

1 source file

When many small help marks sit on a form, arrange them so the set is readable. People should actually be able to read what filling a field does.

## 48. Use a neutral word for all money sources, not only banks

2 source files

When the list includes non-bank lenders, use a neutral word like lenders for the whole set. Keep “bank” only where it truly means a bank — and watch real users before small wording swaps.

## 49. Related results pieces should sit close enough to feel like one

1 source file

Tabs and the table under them should sit close enough to read as one piece. A big empty band between them breaks that feeling.

## 50. Keep a tab switcher that already feels clear

1 source file

If the current full-width tab switcher already feels clear and works, keep it. Do not narrow it onto only the number columns just because another pattern exists.

## 51. Give an obvious control when switching between form and results

1 source file

When the form comes back above the answers, people need an obvious control they can name. They should not wonder how the form rose again.

## 52. Teach the few ways to judge an offer inside the product

1 source file

A home loan is judged from a small set of aspects — rate, amount, length, monthly payment, and charges. Teach those here in the product so people do not have to leave for a long guide.

## 53. Give people a clear start-fresh control on a filled form

1 source file

When a form is already filled, give a clear wipe-and-start-fresh control so people can begin again. People expect that kind of button.

## 54. Stand behind exact numbers with a clear mark

1 source file

Exact rates and amounts inspire confidence. Mark them clearly so the site stands behind those exact figures instead of leaving them unmarked.

## 55. Put the apply action above the row selections

1 source file

The apply action should sit on top of the row selection marks people are using. Selection and apply should feel like one move.

## 56. Say data freshness in clear trust language

1 source file

Replace awkward “data last checked” lines with polished trust wording — such as when it was updated, or that accuracy is stood behind.

## 57. Sort well by default; do not add a search box that adds load

1 source file

Open the list already sorted by rate from lowest to highest. Do not add a name search that piles on mental load — people can use the browser find when they need a name.

## 58. Put important offer facts in the product, not only buried detail

1 source file

Facts a customer could take to a branch manager belong in the main product, not only inside a deep dump or a blog. Static offer facts should be findable without hunting.

## 59. Extra-info cues must be guessable and say what is inside

1 source file

Use a small plus-style cue people can guess, kept compact on phones. The label should say more about the loan offer, not only more about the company name.

## 60. Name every number and show competing limits then the lower one

3 source files

In a how-calculated walk, sequence the steps, carry numbers forward, and name every percent and figure. Show two competing limits clearly, then the lower of them, so a first-time reader can follow.

## 61. Show the math to empower people — do not hide how numbers are made

1 source file

Keep showing how amounts and monthly payments are worked out. Honesty that empowers beats a black-box number from a branch system.

## 62. Footnote marks should look like notes, not links

1 source file

Mark notes with a star, not a dash that looks like a link. Notes must stay notes so people do not think they open somewhere else.

## 63. Fee notes: what it is, that you cannot avoid it, and a short guide

1 source file

A processing-fee note should first define the fee in one sentence, say it is mandatory and due even after approval, then give a short private-versus-public fee guide.

## 64. Warn when the lender will not accept your own report, so you do not pay twice

1 source file

Tell people the lender still runs its own legal, title, and valuation checks and will not accept a report they already paid for. Full honesty here helps them save money.

## 65. Numbered steps should wrap and flow when space is tight

1 source file

When numbered step cards sit in a skinny column, let 1–2–3–4 wrap and flow down with a connecting line. Use the same readable pattern wherever those step walks appear.

## 66. Explain how rates are set in plain advice, not legal jargon

2 source files

Replace walls of legal rate words with short advice on the few ways lenders set rates and when floating or fixed helps. Sound like a friendly lawyer on the customer’s team.

## 67. Show the extra rupees for one missed payment, not only a yearly percent

2 source files

After one missed monthly payment, show the extra rupees, bounce fee, and how many days until notice or collection. A yearly percent alone is the wrong unit for that month.

## 68. Warn about high miss-payment penalties before people choose

1 source file

Label lenders by how harsh the miss-payment penalty is, not only raw numbers. Warn before choice: do not pick a lender where one miss costs double for a tiny rate edge.

## 69. When several plans apply, pick the best and hide the plan name

1 source file

If someone qualifies for more than one plan from the same lender, pick the best one and show that offer. Do not force people to choose by plan name.
