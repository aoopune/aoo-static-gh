## Exact solution (or idea that can also be a solution)
They applied Google Flights to this bank list: “How does it work in Google Flights? Prices for this flight is low at this moment. … The list of flights is written here. These prices are high for this time. They are unlikely to go down.” One speaker: “We think it's okay. We don't get scammed. … It's definitely a competitive advantage.” That honesty line is the idea that can also be the solution for rate context on Explore banks. The other speaker disagreed: “I don't know about Google Flight. When they say prices are low, I feel like I am being fooled.” Then they used Amazon and a juicer sale as the opposite method — hype, fake discount, Prime Day “you will never get the same sales,” later a lower price — “It is not about the sale. It is about making a fool out of the customer.” Shroffin should not do that to the customer. Disagreement is part of the same finding; they did not pick a winner.

## What this is for
Honest context on whether the rates shown in Bank options (Canara Bank row, Rate column redacted in screenshots) are low, high, or unlikely to fall. Issue-01 names the missing layer; this file is the borrowed honesty pattern and the anti-hype company rule.

## Why they said it that way
Trust and not scamming the Indian user. Competitive advantage if honesty is written. Fear of being fooled when a site says “prices are low,” learned from Amazon sales and a juicer company’s year-long “we will double the difference” promise they do not trust.
