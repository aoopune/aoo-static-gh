# “Zero commissions” mixes bank and customer, so the words are unclear

They like the intent: independent platform, no pay to push a bank, no built-in bias.
They do not think a customer will understand “commission” here — it sounds like “you pay us nothing,” not “we take nothing from the bank.”
Then “Zero bias” asks you to switch meaning. The block never stays in one relationship.
The small line under the heading is too small and does not carry the gist (fair view, no one pushed, no one can pay to change ranking).

## Exact theme

The finished Zero heading is **Zero commissions. Zero bias.** plus a small fair-view line. They like independence (no commission from anyone to push a bank). They do not think “commission” is clear. Quote: “But this zero commissions, I don't think it comes across easily to the customer.” “Instead of thinking that they are earning no commission from the bank.” “But perspective should not shift.”

As a customer, “commissions” first sounds like a fee taken from you. Then “Zero bias” means not pushing any bank. Quote: “when I read the word commissions, I feel, instantly as a consumer, I don't know which commission I am talking about.” “Somewhere, it should be said that either this whole section is about website and bank, or this whole section is about website and customer. It should not be mixed in the context.”

They do not want that mix to make people ask how Shroffin earns, including “do they sell data?” They want one customer-facing wording that still says no earnings from banks and no push.

The supporting sentence is too small and “listed on a platform” could be said better. The gist they wanted in the last sentence: fair view of lenders in the market, ranked fairly, no one pushed forward, no one can pay to change the ranking — in simple polished English, not big words.
