# Guide intelligence — numbered bullets (Phase 2 wire format)

Rule: **related sentences stay in one bullet** so meaning stays intact.

Source: `_guide-intelligence-founder-choices-ledger.json` (`chosen_bullets`)


## Overview

### 1. `#loan-amount`

1. The property-side number often looks bigger first; the income cap is usually what shrinks the loan once EMIs and card limits are in.
2. If you need more headroom, a real earning co-applicant who signs is the clean fix — fudging names or income is where files stall at verification.

### 2. `#emi`

1. A slightly lower interest rate can still cost you more if you miss one EMI. Overdue charges on home loans range from about 0% to 24% a year depending on the bank.
2. In the early years you are mostly paying interest, so one miss costs more than the monthly EMI suggests. Check the miss charge on the Key Fact Statement, not just the rate.

### 3. `#tenure`

1. A brochure may say 30 years, but your age at the end of the loan can shorten that — ask the bank what tenure someone your age actually gets.
2. A longer tenure means a smaller EMI and usually more total interest. If your income rises later, cutting the tenure is a simple way to reduce that extra interest.

### 4. `#rates`

1. Most banks offer floating; fewer list fixed — and many applications still end on floating at sanction even when a bank's fixed offering exists on paper.
2. Fixed only helps if you expect rates to rise over the next few years and you need the EMI to stay the same with no room in your budget for a hike. If you may prepay or expect rates to fall, floating is usually cheaper.

### 5. `#loan-structure`

1. Overdraft only saves money if surplus cash stays in the linked account — money you take out does not reduce interest. Some lenders also charge when you use too little of the limit, or extra interest if you draw above the operating limit.
2. Without that discipline, a term loan with part-prepayment when you can is simpler. Ask how dropline and utilisation charges work before you choose.

### 6. `#charges`

1. If you already paid a lawyer, the bank still runs its own property checks — your report does not replace theirs. Processing fees are sometimes waived or reduced; legal, valuation, and government registration fees usually are not.
2. A small login fee does not mean cheap overall. Read every line on the Key Fact Statement before you pay anything upfront.

### 7. `#project-bank-approval`

1. A builder may say every bank is on board — that is not the same as your chosen bank having APF for that project today. Confirm with the bank you plan to use before you pay login fees, especially if you are choosing between two projects.
2. Resale is checked unit by unit anyway, so expect a longer property review there.


## Documents

### 8. `#kyc`

1. KYC usually stalls when your name or address does not match across PAN, your photo ID, and the loan form — not because you picked the wrong ID from the list. Line those three up before you submit.
2. Every co-applicant needs their own set of papers; adding someone who is not really earning or on the loan just to fill the form creates problems later, not a stronger file.

### 9. `#income`

1. Banks lend against the income you can prove on paper — salaried and self-employed follow different paths, and the bank can match salary credits in your statement against your slips. Variable pay and bonuses may count for less than your monthly slip suggests.
2. A co-applicant only raises the loan when their income is real, documented the same way, and visible in bank credits — not cash you cannot trace.

### 10. `#property`

1. Your sale papers open the file — the bank still runs its own legal and valuation check, and a report you paid for privately does not replace that. On resale, gaps in the title chain stall more files than one missing receipt.
2. On a new home, the agreement value on your cost sheet is what the bank lends against — not a verbal discount from the builder.

### 11. `#other`

1. Insurance and other add-ons are separate from the processing fee — someone at the bank may still try to bundle them in. Even after a sanction letter, the processing fee still applies if you choose to proceed, and it is usually non-refundable.
2. As per RBI KFS guidelines, any charge not listed in the Key Fact Statement cannot be taken without your clear consent — read the KFS before you write the cheque. Pay only what is named there; question extras you did not ask for.


## Tax benefits

### 12. `#interest`

1. Start with your lender's interest certificate, not your EMI — deductible interest is the interest portion on that paper, and it must match what you file.
2. ₹2 lakh is the deduction limit on the old regime for a self-occupied home; the tax you save is that amount times your slab — so at a 30% slab the saving is up to about ₹60,000, not ₹2 lakh back.
3. If you are on the new regime and live in the home, this deduction usually does not apply — pick your regime before you count on it.

### 13. `#principal`

1. Home loan principal only helps on tax if space is left in your ₹1.5 lakh 80C limit — EPF from salary often fills most of it first. If the limit is already used up, more principal in your EMI saves no tax that year.
2. You can start claiming only from the possession year. Sell the home within five years after that and the principal benefit you took can be added back to your income.

### 14. `#under-construction`

1. You pay interest during construction, but that amount cannot go into the tax return for the year you paid it.
2. After you get the keys, the built-up pre-possession total is claimed in five equal parts — one part each year for five years. Many people file too early because they see interest leaving the bank every month.

### 15. `#joint-loan`

1. To claim together, both names must be on the property deed and the loan — a loan name alone is not enough. The bank gives one interest certificate; each person files their share in their own return, not the full amount twice.
2. Old regime only — up to ₹2 lakh interest and ₹1.5 lakh 80C per person on that share.

### 16. `#how-to-claim`

1. Interest and principal are claimed separately on your tax return — not in the same place. Use the split on your lender's interest certificate, not the EMI amount that left your bank.

### 17. `#other`

1. Stamp duty, principal, and insurance you paid yourself share one ₹1.5 lakh 80C limit.
2. On the old regime, interest loss on a self-occupied home can offset salary by up to ₹2 lakh that year — the rest carries forward.
3. The older first-home extras apply only if your sanction letter is dated in those listed windows.


## Concessions

### 18. `#bank-rates`

1. Before you restructure the loan for a woman's name, check whether your shortlisted bank actually offers that cut — only some lenders do, and it is usually about 0.05%. On a ₹50 lakh loan over 20 years that saves roughly ₹150 a month.
2. A lower base rate from another bank can beat that without changing who is primary.

### 19. `#pmay`

1. The subsidy trims interest on the first ₹8 lakh of your loan — not on the full amount you borrow. That is why the saving tops out at about ₹1.80 lakh, even when your loan is ₹25 lakh.

### 20. `#fees`

1. A free processing fee does not mean free overall. Legal, valuation, GST, and stamp duty often still apply. Read every fee line on the Key Fact Statement before you pay.


## Insurance hub

### 21. `#cover-types`

1. Property cover fixes the house after fire or flood. Loan cover clears the outstanding loan if the borrower dies. They solve different problems — one policy usually does not do both jobs.

### 22. `#must-buy`

1. You usually need property cover on the house for as long as the loan runs.
2. You usually do not need the bank's loan cover if your term plan already covers the full loan — only buy it when you lack that cover.

### 23. `#key-points`

1. If you may prepay or switch banks within a few years, ask the refund on bank loan cover first — single-premium products often return little.
2. If you will keep the loan long-term, then compare term plan vs loan cover on total cost.


## Property cover

### 24. `#coverage`

1. Property cover pays to rebuild the structure — not the flat's market price if it falls, and not the land underneath. Insure for reconstruction cost, not the headline price you paid the builder.

### 25. `#setup`

1. The cover amount should match rebuild cost — not the bank's loan valuation or the price you paid for flat plus land. Using the wrong number leaves you under-insured without noticing.

### 26. `#pay`

1. Property premium is usually smaller than loan cover — but if it still goes into your loan, you pay interest on it every month. Pay the insurer directly when the bank allows.

### 27. `#claim`

1. If the house is a total loss, the building insurance payout often pays the bank first — up to what you still owe on the loan. Anything left after that comes to you. That is normal when the bank is named on the policy.


## Loan cover

### 28. `#coverage`

1. If people rely on your income for this EMI, you need death cover for at least the outstanding loan. Loan cover pays the bank directly; a term plan pays your family, who can clear the loan or use the money elsewhere.
2. You usually only need one for the full loan amount.

### 29. `#setup`

1. If clearing the loan is the only goal, reducing cover (tracks your outstanding balance) is usually enough — you are not paying for cover on money you have already repaid.
2. Level cover costs more; choose it only if your family needs more than what is still owed on the loan.

### 30. `#pay`

1. If you buy loan cover, paying the premium outside the loan keeps interest off that amount — when the bank allows it. If they add it to your principal, you repay it with interest for the full loan term.

### 31. `#claim`

1. If your family would need cash after the loan is cleared, loan cover may not be the right product — the payout goes to the bank first on your Certificate of Insurance, and the nominee only gets what is left if the certificate allows it.

### 32. `#compare`

1. If you can get a term plan for the full loan amount, compare that first — the nominee gets paid, the cover can outlast the loan, and it usually stays if you prepay or switch.
2. Loan cover (bank paid first, often reducing cover) is mainly worth it when age or health makes term hard to get.


## Complaints

### 33. `#path`

1. RBI (bank) and NHB (housing finance company) usually come after your lender's own complaint officers on the lender's website — nodal officer, grievance redressal officer. Going to the regulator too early often gets the file sent back.

### 34. `#talk`

1. If the loan team fixes it on the first contact, ask for written confirmation by email — otherwise you have no proof the issue was closed if it comes back.

### 35. `#write`

1. Ask for one fix — money back, rate corrected, papers returned.
2. If the fight is about a fee or rate, point to the line on your Key Fact Statement or sanction letter that shows what you were promised.

### 36. `#stuck`

1. You usually have about 90 days after the lender's last reply — or after the 30-day wait ended with no reply — to file with RBI or NHB. Miss that window and the door closes.

### 37. `#limits`

1. This path fixes how the lender handled your case — it will not force a refused loan or a lower rate on its own. If you only want a better rate, compare lenders instead.

### 38. `#contacts`

1. Before you open RBI CMS (bank) or NHB GRIDS (housing finance company), gather your loan number, the lender's complaint ticket, dates, and your Key Fact Statement or sanction letter — incomplete filings get rejected.
