### card: interest
**On-page title:** Can you claim the interest on tax return?
**Label:** Before you decide
**Confidence:** high
**Sources used:** tax-benefits.body.html; wb-rec-260815-2213 Flights honesty — ceiling ≠ refund

**Paste-ready intelligence**

The ₹2 lakh figure people quote is a ceiling under the old regime for a self-occupied home — not a promise of money back in your account.

Whether you live there or rent it out, and which tax regime you’re on, changes the story more than the headline number. Claim only what your lender’s interest certificate shows for the year.

**Worked example:** none

**Must not say**
- Retell full ₹2 lakh / ₹30,000 / new-regime grid
- “You will get ₹2 lakh”

### card: principal
**On-page title:** Can you claim the principal on tax return?
**Label:** Watch for
**Confidence:** high
**Sources used:** tax-benefits.body.html; startup-core

**Paste-ready intelligence**

Principal doesn’t get its own private pocket — it shares the old-regime 80C basket with EPF, PPF, insurance premiums, and the rest.

If that basket is already full, more EMI principal won’t create room. Possession is when this claim usually starts; sell within five years and what you claimed can come back as taxable.

**Worked example:** none

**Must not say**
- Full 80C encyclopedia again
- That principal works under the new regime

**Fits existing UI as:** Soft callout after the hero answer.

### card: under-construction
**On-page title:** What if the home is under construction?
**Label:** Watch for
**Confidence:** high
**Sources used:** tax-benefits.body.html; standing_practice — timing trap

**Paste-ready intelligence**

Paying interest while the building goes up doesn’t mean you claim it that same year. The usual story starts from the year you get the keys, with pre-possession interest spread across five years after that.

Filing early on under-construction interest is one of the more common quiet mistakes on this page.

**Worked example:** none

**Must not say**
- Retell the five-instalment math in full
- Claim the whole pile in year one

### card: joint-loan
**On-page title:** Can both co-owners claim tax benefits?
**Label:** Before you decide
**Confidence:** medium
**Sources used:** wb-rec-260815-2213/solution-04 — honest spouse structure only

**Paste-ready intelligence**

A joint loan isn’t an automatic double benefit. Two people claim when both names sit on the property and the loan, and each claims only what they actually paid within their ownership share.

Putting a spouse on for a real shared purchase is one thing. Inventing a co-owner for tax theatre is another — the interest certificate and the payment trail will show it.

**Worked example:** none

**Must not say**
- “Joint doubles every ceiling”
- Fake co-ownership coaching

**Fits existing UI as:** Soft callout under the joint rule.

**Explore handoff:** Explore for eligibility; this line is tax honesty.

### card: how-to-claim

**Label:** Your move
**Confidence:** high
**Sources used:** tax-benefits.body.html; wb-rec-260815-2213 — paper that wins

**Paste-ready intelligence**

Interest and principal don’t go in the same box on the return — wrong section is a common miss even when the rupees are right.

This year’s interest certificate is the paper that carries the split. The calculator on this card is a check, not your filed amount.

### card: other
**On-page title:** Anything else on tax?
**Label:** Watch for
**Confidence:** high
**Sources used:** tax-benefits.body.html; wb-rec-260815-2213 — don’t sell old windows as current

**Paste-ready intelligence**

Most of the “extras” on this card only help if your sanction year and who paid the premium actually match — older first-home windows don’t quietly apply to every new loan.

Premium the lender folds into EMI usually doesn’t count as something you paid yourself for 80C. When in doubt, match the disclosure to your facts and ignore the rest.

**Worked example:** none

- Promise old extras on post-2022 sanctions
