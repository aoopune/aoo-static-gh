### card: cover-types
**On-page title:** Two covers
**Label:** Before you decide
**Confidence:** high
**Sources used:** home-loan-insurance.body.html; wb-rec-260815-2213

**Paste-ready intelligence**

These are two different fears wearing the same word “insurance.” One is about the house getting damaged; the other is about the loan still owing if someone dies.

Buying the wrong one for the fear you actually have wastes premium and leaves the other gap open. The Certificate of Insurance or Policy Schedule is what you bought — not the sales chat.

**Worked example:** none

**Must not say**
- Retell both product definitions at length
- That one policy does both jobs automatically

**Fits existing UI as:** Soft callout under the two tiles.

**Explore handoff:** none

### card: must-buy
**On-page title:** Must you buy insurance?
**Label:** Ask the lender
**Confidence:** high
**Sources used:** RBI voluntary wording on page; wb-rec-260815-2213 squeeze bundling; data insurance pricing ≠ must-buy

**Paste-ready intelligence**

Property cover is often written into the loan while it runs. Loan cover is usually optional — and optional still isn’t free.

RBI’s line on this page is clear enough: insurance through the bank’s agency is voluntary and shouldn’t be tied to getting the loan. When a desk bundles a policy into sanction, the useful question is whether disbursement actually depends on it. Some lenders on our sheet also tweak the listed rate when insurance pricing applies (172 of 806 offers) — that’s a pricing rule, not proof you must take their partner product.

**Worked example:** none

**Must not say**
- Re-quote the full RBI paragraph
- Villain framing of bank staff; “must buy partner policy”

**Fits existing UI as:** Soft callout beside the status grid.

**Explore handoff:** Explore may show rate-with-insurance pricing — still not a must-buy rule.

### card: key-points
**On-page title:** What to check before you buy?
**Label:** Watch for
**Confidence:** high
**Sources used:** founder digest — pay insurance outside; hub page; theme-12

**Paste-ready intelligence**

If the premium sits inside the loan, you pay interest on the premium too — that’s the quiet cost people miss while nodding at “included.”

Paying it separately, when the bank allows, keeps that interest off the table. For the death-of-borrower job, a term plan is often cheaper than bank loan cover for the same need; and early close can end cover with only a partial refund, or none. I’d want those three answers on paper before I signed a long single-premium add-on.

**Worked example:** none

**Must not say**
- Exact rupee savings without the visitor’s inputs
- That every bank must accept outside payment

**Fits existing UI as:** Soft callout under the three-step flow.

**Explore handoff:** After Compare, see if a listed rate assumes insurance pricing.
