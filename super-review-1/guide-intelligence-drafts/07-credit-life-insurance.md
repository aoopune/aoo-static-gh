### card: coverage
**On-page title:** What does it pay for?
**Label:** Before you decide
**Confidence:** high
**Sources used:** credit-life-insurance.body.html; hub sibling

**Paste-ready intelligence**

Loan cover is built around the bank’s unpaid balance if the named borrower dies — it isn’t a full family life payout dressed as a home-loan add-on.

Only events named on the Certificate of Insurance count. If you already hold a term plan, some banks will take an assignment of that cover instead of selling you a fresh policy; that’s worth knowing before you nod at the desk product.

**Worked example:** none

**Must not say**
- Retell covered/not columns
- Guaranteed “best cover”

### card: setup
**On-page title:** How is the cover set up?
**Label:** Watch for
**Confidence:** high
**Sources used:** credit-life-insurance.body.html; wb-rec-260815-2213 paper that wins

**Paste-ready intelligence**

If this page and the Certificate of Insurance disagree, the certificate wins — that’s the written deal.

Cover usually starts when the loan is disbursed and the insurer is paid. A mismatched loan account number, or reducing cover you thought was level, is the kind of quiet gap that only shows up when someone needs it.

### card: pay
**On-page title:** How big is the premium?
**Label:** Common trap
**Confidence:** high
**Sources used:** founder digest pay outside; hub; wb-rec-260815-2213 Flights honesty

**Paste-ready intelligence**

Loan-cover premiums already tend to cost more than property cover. Folding that premium into the loan means you also pay interest on it for years — same cover, heavier bill.

Buying stays voluntary under RBI rules; clear consent matters. Outside the loan first is usually the cleaner money path when cash allows. Anything that slips insurance into principal belongs on the sanction letter and Key Fact Statement before you sign, not after.

**Worked example:** none

**Must not say**
- Exact premiums for named insurers
- That bundling is “included free”

### card: claim
**On-page title:** How does a claim work?
**Label:** Your move
**Confidence:** high
**Sources used:** credit-life-insurance.body.html; wb-rec-260815-2213

**Paste-ready intelligence**

On a claim, the insurer pays the bank first, up to the outstanding loan. The family isn’t first in line the way they are on a normal term plan — and leftover to a nominee exists only when the certificate allows it.

Many plans pay the certificate amount, not today’s exact balance, unless the papers say the live balance tracks. Early close or a transfer often can’t take this cover with you, and refunds can be partial or zero. That’s the trade people miss when the add-on feels convenient at sanction.

**Worked example:** none

**Must not say**
- Nominee always gets leftover
- Guaranteed full refund on foreclosure

**Fits existing UI as:** Soft callout on Claim.

### card: compare
**On-page title:** Term plan or loan cover?
**Label:** Before you decide
**Confidence:** medium
**Sources used:** hub — term often cheaper; startup-core no guaranteed best; Guide compare strip

**Paste-ready intelligence**

Term plan and loan cover both clear a loan on death — they just don’t treat your family, your future switches, or your premium the same way.

A term plan usually costs less per rupee of cover, can outlast the loan, and pays your nominee, who can clear the bank and keep the rest. Loan cover is often single-premium, usually shrinks as you repay, often can’t move on transfer, and pays the bank first. If age or health makes term hard to buy, loan cover can be easier to place — ease still isn’t proof it’s cheaper over the full term. There isn’t a guaranteed best policy for everyone; the useful compare is premium-out, who gets paid, and whether the cover can travel.

**Worked example:** none

**Must not say**
- “Always buy term” / “always refuse loan cover”
- Invented premium bands as fact
