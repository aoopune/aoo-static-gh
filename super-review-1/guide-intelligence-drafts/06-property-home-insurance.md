### card: coverage
**On-page title:** What does it pay for?
**Label:** Before you decide
**Confidence:** high
**Sources used:** property-home-insurance.body.html; startup-core

**Paste-ready intelligence**

This cover is about repairing or rebuilding the structure after listed damage — not about the flat’s market price going up or down, and not about the land under it.

Until possession, risk on an unfinished house usually sits with the seller or builder anyway. What actually pays is whatever the Policy Schedule names; sales talk doesn’t expand the list.

**Worked example:** none

**Must not say**
- Re-list every peril tab
- Invented “full replacement guaranteed”

**Fits existing UI as:** Soft callout under the chapter rule.

**Explore handoff:** none

### card: setup
**On-page title:** How is the cover set up?
**Label:** Watch for
**Confidence:** high
**Sources used:** property-home-insurance.body.html; wb-rec-260816-0004 honesty

**Paste-ready intelligence**

The sum that matters is rebuild cost of the structure — not the sale price of flat-plus-land. Using the bank’s loan valuation as if it were rebuild cost is how people end up under-insured without noticing.

Some products pay to restore the home; others only clear what you still owe the bank. Those are different promises wearing similar jackets. The Policy Schedule line for sum insured and product type is where that difference shows up.

**Worked example:** none

**Must not say**
- Exact rebuild formula for every city
- That bank valuation equals rebuild automatically

**Fits existing UI as:** Soft callout after What to check.

**Explore handoff:** none

### card: pay
**On-page title:** How big is the premium?
**Label:** Ask the lender
**Confidence:** medium
**Sources used:** hub sibling pay-outside; sheet has non-renewal penalties at some lenders; 18% GST on page

**Paste-ready intelligence**

Property premium is usually smaller than loan cover, but clubbing it into the home loan still means interest on that premium.

Partner policies aren’t automatically the best fit — the hub already notes you can often choose an insurer outside the list. A lapsed policy leaves the house naked, and some lenders even list a separate hit for non-renewal. Amount, inside-vs-outside the loan, and renewal with the bank still named — those three answers usually decide whether the premium is quiet or expensive.

**Worked example:** none

**Must not say**
- Invented premium bands
- Guaranteed cheapest; villain bank staff

**Fits existing UI as:** Soft callout on Premium.

**Explore handoff:** Explore Charges for non-renewal penalties on compared banks.

### card: claim
**On-page title:** What happens after damage?
**Label:** Your move
**Confidence:** high
**Sources used:** property-home-insurance.body.html; wb-rec-260815-2018

**Paste-ready intelligence**

After damage, the file moves money — not a reassuring phone call. Photos, reports, and the Policy Schedule in one place beat a scattered WhatsApp thread later.

On total loss, the bank is often named on the building payout under an Agreed Bank Clause. That’s normal mortgage reality, not a surprise betrayal — worth knowing before you assume the cheque comes only to you.
