## Exact solution (or idea that can also be a solution)

“**Google shows the flights.** They show the flights that **these prices are low.**” User story: ticket to Bangalore from Chennai, Coimbatore, Mumbai — “They told me that these prices are low in Bangalore. **I have a lot of confidence.**” Rhetorical: “Do you use AI? Do you check the old data? **It is a good thing.**”

**Applied direction for Shroffin:** Explore banks tips / rate context should read like **honest product judgment** (this offer is low / this is your closest offer / gap to better offer) the way Google states low prices — **not** an AI demo.

## What this is for

**Explore banks** copy and UX for the future **top-three optimizations** block — how the user should *feel* about offer quality (confidence, not suspicion).

## Why they said it that way

Users don't need the machinery; they need **clear, trustworthy labels** on whether a number is good. Google Flights is the positive mirror of “never mention AI” (solution-02).
