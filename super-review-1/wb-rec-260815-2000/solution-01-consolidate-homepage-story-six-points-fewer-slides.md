## Exact solution (or idea that can also be a solution)

They want the homepage story refactored, not expanded. After walking the four full-screen story regions (Transparent; Zero commissions/bias; Best of all / no spam; plus the opening re-engineered block), they counted **six unique points** total and said the first half is not unique while the second half can be accommodated. The constructive direction:

- **Do not give “Transparent, like never before” its own full slide** — it is self-explanatory and has nothing else on screen; maybe skip the section, add it later, or treat it differently.
- **Drop the extra line** “So you find what works for you — with no surprises” / “You don't have to add anything” on that slide — they said you don't have to add anything.
- **Merge or cut repeats**: “Zero commission, zero bias” is already said; browse-at-own-pace / no phone number is already said; spam/no hard sell overlaps with earlier copy.
- **Keep the four “Best of all…” points** (spam, hard sell, notifications, pick banks / apply once) as the genuinely new information in that stretch — but still fold them into fewer slides, not four separate full screens.
- **Grouping sketch**: “Make a slide of these two. Make a slide of these three. And make a slide of the last point.”
- **Constraints**: one screen does not take too many words; decide minimum words per point, which points go together, and how they flow; **by reducing section** without making the homepage long.
- **Named goal**: “The goal of entire activity is consolidation.” “Whatever you are making, we have to increase it. We have to reduce it.”
- **Keep what works**: “Overall, I like the font, color guide, sections and spacing. I like it a lot.”

While reviewing the Zero slide fade they also said **“I think we should remove this section”** twice — a potential cut tied to issue-02, still within the consolidation frame.

## Why they said it that way

Each full-screen slide currently tells them their own qualities again instead of advancing the story. Transparency in the middle of four texts is a concern even if the words are good. Putting everything in one place becomes heavy, so the page was split — but the split went too far. They want fewer sections with the same six facts, not a longer homepage.
