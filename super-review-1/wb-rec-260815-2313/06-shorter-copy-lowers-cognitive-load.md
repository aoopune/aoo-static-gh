## First-principles problem
- What must be true: the information (no commission, no bias, compare, apply, banks compete) can stay. The **shape** must match how people already parse language so the brain does not load extra context or extra “no”s or extra cause–effect.
- Root vs symptom: “too much copy” is the symptom. The root is **repeated operators** (no, no, no), **stacked claims** (priority / sponsorship), and **spelled-out causation** (“once you X, then Y”) when the short pair already holds both ideas.
- Constraints: don’t drop the facts; drop the extra machinery. Fit “normal thinking process.”

## Directions they considered
- Keep **No commission. No bias.** as the first structure; treat further claims (priority treatment, sponsorship) as load.
- Prefer **No x, y, z** over **No x, no y, no z**. Lean: the extra “no”s are the waste.
- Prefer **Compare and apply. Let banks compete.** over **Compare all and apply in one shot. And banks will compete for you.** Lean: short form **decreases** load.
- Rejected: making the listener reconstruct “banks compete **because** you compared and applied” as a separate step.
