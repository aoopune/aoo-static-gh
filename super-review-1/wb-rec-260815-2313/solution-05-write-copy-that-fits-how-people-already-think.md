They said extra “no”s and extra clauses raise the load; shorter lines lower it.

## Exact solution (or idea that can also be a solution)
There is a cognitive load. First sentences we had — Structure, No commission, No bias — increase it because they load a lot of context. “No x, y, z” versus “No x, no y, no z”: the extra nos increase load (ASR “two 9 extra” = two extra nos). We have to make words that fit how people think; we don’t have to increase the load; give information that fits that. Concrete rewrite: “Compare all and apply in one shot. And banks will compete for you” increases load; “Compare and apply. Let banks compete.” decreases it. Same idea as modern training data: common practices need less training; custom instructions need more; models follow familiar instructions (open the PR, then write the code, then open the PR — ASR order is scrambled) and the load of the model is reduced. Then: people usually train left to right, up to down; we do the same (also restates solution 01).

## What this is for
Shroffin sentence-writing, including early claims and the compare/apply promise. They searched Google for “cognitive load” to name the idea. `issue-04-copy-extra-words-raise-cognitive-load.md` is the defect of extra words; this file is the shorter-copy direction.

## Why they said it that way
People already have a way to understand language. Extra words make them put in a lot of effort. Cognitive load is the borrowed name (Google). Training-data / common instructions is the engineering analogy.
