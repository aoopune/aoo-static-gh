## Exact solution (or idea that can also be a solution)

They already had the **Loan amount** drawer open (same Bank of Maharashtra · Maha Super Housing Loan drawer as the previous recording). On screen the six cards print **80.00%**, **55.00%**, **10.00%**, and **−₹555** on the arithmetic lines.

Joined direction: **sequence the steps** so the ₹55,000 from income allowance is carried into the next box, and **name every number**. Raw ASR: “We need to arrange the viewers. Step 1. Step 2. If we can carry 55,000, we can do the next step 3.” (ASR *viewers*; screen is FOIR / income-allowance ₹55,000.) After clicking steps 1–5: “I followed 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. So I don't know why the next step is useful.” “Now he has done into 800. What is 800?” (screen **80.00%**.) “And then 75. … Or did he multiply it with 55?” (screen **55.00%**.) They restated the intended chain: income allowance × FOIR (ASR *fire*) minus existing EMIs minus credit-card load = monthly EMI eligibility; income limit ~68 lakh; lower-of → 48 lakh. Closing ask: “Sequencing should be done. I need to follow all these calculations. I need their names and labels. … It should be very easy.” That is both an **idea** they discussed and the **proposed change** for this drawer.

## What this is for

The Bank of Maharashtra loan-amount how-calculated drawer on Explore banks — so a customer can follow why the table shows ₹48,00,000. Defect file `issue-01-loan-amount-how-calculated-steps-unlabeled-unsequenced.md` is the unlabeled steps; this file is the direction (sequence + names/labels).

## Why they said it that way

They were walking the math as a first-time reader. Bare 80 / 55 / 10 / 555 looked like mystery multipliers. Convenience: “It should be very easy.” They even allowed collapsing to one calculation if the remaining one can be followed.
