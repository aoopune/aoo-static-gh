Property cover

Without it, a fire or flood can leave you paying the EMI and rebuilding at the same time.

  • What it does
  • When
  • Decisions
  • Before you pay

The one thing this does

It pays to repair or rebuild the house after listed events — fire, flood, earthquake, storm, and similar. Contents only if you add them.

It does not clear the loan if you die. That is loan cover. If you only buy the wrong product, the house can still be gone and the EMI still due.

When you take it

Property cover is for a house that is already built and in your hands. Take it when you get possession — not while the building is still under construction.

Until handover is complete, risk for the unfinished house usually sits with the seller or builder. That is typically written in the sale agreement. Buying dwelling property cover early does not fix that stage.

Ready-to-move or resale with possession now: insure then. Under construction: wait for possession, then insure.

Why you need to care

The home is usually your largest purchase. Damage without insurance means a double burden: the loan continues, and repair money has to come from somewhere else.

Many loan agreements require the house to stay insured while the loan runs. That protects you and the lender’s security. It does not mean you must buy only their partner brand — any authorised insurer that meets the agreement is usually fine.

The decisions that change everything

Is this the right moment?

Only after possession of a finished house. Under construction is not what this policy is for.

How large is the cover?

Use rebuild cost of the structure — not market price of flat plus land. Too low and the lender may reject the policy. Land is not insured.

Building, contents, or both?

Ask what your agreement expects. Jewellery and art are usually separate choices, not in the default contents pot.

Is the lender named?

On a mortgaged home, wrong or missing bank naming is a common reason papers bounce — at disbursement or at claim time.

Will this last as long as the loan?

Standard dwelling cover is often up to about ten years. Longer loans need renewals with the lender still named.1

Rebuild cover or loan-linked package?

Some packages pay toward outstanding loan, not full rebuild. Know which promise you are buying.2

What usually pays — and what does not

Typical covered events: fire, lightning, earthquake, storm, flood, and other listed perils. Theft is usually only shortly after another covered event — not ordinary burglary alone.

Typical gaps: deliberate damage, war and nuclear risks, items taken out of the home, drop in market value, and using the home as something other than a residence.

If something happens

Make safe. Tell the insurer quickly. Keep damaged property for survey if asked. When the lender is named, expect them on the building settlement. Payout follows rebuild rules — not land-inclusive market price.

Before you pay

Open the Policy Schedule. If these are wrong, stop.

  • Possession of a finished house (not under construction)
  • Address of the house
  • Sum insured on a rebuild basis
  • Building and/or contents — as you intended
  • Lender named correctly
  • Period of cover and renewal plan
  • Premium
