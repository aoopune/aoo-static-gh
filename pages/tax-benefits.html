Your home loan EMI has tax benefits.

Know how much of this loan can still reduce your tax this year.

Contents

  • 02 Principal repayment
  • 03 Under construction
  • 04 Joint loan

01

Interest payment

Interest payment Section 22↗ (opens official Income Tax page)

The limit depends on whether you live in the home or rent it out.

You live in it Self-occupied

  • Old tax regime Up to ₹2 lakh a year.
  • New tax regime Cannot be claimed.
  • ₹2 lakh limit Needs a loan to buy or build, and the home ready within 5 years. If either is missing, only ₹30,000 a year.
  • Loan taken for repair, renewal or reconstruction Only ₹30,000 a year.

You rent it out Let-out

  • Old tax regime No fixed upper cap.
  • New tax regime Can be claimed against rental income.
  • 5-year ready rule Does not apply when you rent it out.

Up to two homes can be treated as self-occupied.

02

Principal repayment

Principal repayment Schedule XV↗ (opens official Income Tax page)

Available under the old tax regime only. Same whether you live in the home or rent it out.

  • Limit Up to ₹1.5 lakh a year.
  • Shared with EPF, life insurance, and other eligible items.
  • When it starts After construction is complete and you have possession.
  • Sale within 5 years Earlier principal repayment claims are added back to income. Interest payment under Section 22 is not reversed the same way.
03

Timing

Under construction

Interest paid before possession is claimed from the year you get possession.

  • When you get possession Claim that earlier interest in 5 equal yearly parts.
  • Interest after possession Claim each year as usual.
  • If you live there (old tax regime) Pre-possession and after-possession interest together stay inside the ₹2 lakh limit.
  • If you rent it out The ₹2 lakh limit does not apply.
04

Co-owners

Joint home loan

Each person can claim their own interest payment and principal repayment limits if they are on the property papers, on the loan, and share the EMI. Each claims in their ownership share.

05

Filing

How to claim

Interest payment and principal repayment go in different places on your return.

  • Who can claim Your name must be on the property papers — being on the loan alone is not enough.
  • Interest payment Claimed under Income from house property.
  • Principal repayment Claimed as a deduction from total income.
  • If you get a salary Share the interest payment and principal repayment figures with your employer for TDS.

Documents you need

Interest certificate

From the lender for the year. Shows interest payment and principal repayment split.

Possession proof

Needed when principal repayment and pre-possession interest payment start.

06

Also worth knowing

Other

Related tax points that are not part of your EMI interest payment or principal repayment.

  • Stamp duty and registration Only for the property purchase transaction. Can be claimed in the year you pay them, within the same ₹1.5 lakh principal repayment basket. Not loan costs.
  • Home loan protection insurance Premium can sit in the same ₹1.5 lakh basket if you pay it yourself — not if the lender adds it to your EMI.
  • House property loss (old tax regime) If interest creates a loss, only up to ₹2 lakh can be set off against other income like salary in that year. The rest can be carried forward.
  • House property loss (new tax regime) Interest on a rented home can still reduce rental income. A loss from this usually cannot cut other income like salary.
  • Extra first-time buyer interest Under the old tax regime only, you may claim extra interest under Section 130↗ (opens official Income Tax page) (loan sanctioned 1 April 2016–31 March 2017; up to ₹50,000 extra; loan up to ₹35 lakh; house up to ₹50 lakh) or Section 131↗ (opens official Income Tax page) (loan sanctioned 1 April 2019–31 March 2022; up to ₹1.5 lakh extra; stamp value up to ₹45 lakh). This sits over and above the Section 22 interest limit, only if this was your first home. You cannot claim both. Not for loans sanctioned after 31 March 2022.
  • Plot or land-only loan Principal repayment usually starts only after the house is built.

For general understanding only. Home loan tax rules depend on your facts, the tax regime you choose, and current law. Confirm claims with a qualified tax professional before you file.
