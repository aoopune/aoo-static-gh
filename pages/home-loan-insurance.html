01

Two covers

If the house is damaged

Property cover

Pays to repair or rebuild the house after listed damage — fire, flood, and similar events on the policy.

Lenders ask so fire or flood does not wipe out the house that secures the loan.

See property cover

On death of the Life Assured

Loan cover

Pays toward the outstanding home loan on death of the Life Assured — and for critical illness, disability, or accident only if those are on the Certificate of Insurance.

Lenders ask so the outstanding loan can still be paid on death of the Life Assured.

See loan cover
02

Requirement

Must you buy it?

Property cover

Often written into the loan agreement while the loan runs.

Loan cover

Usually optional.

For both, the insurer can usually be chosen — not only the bank's partner.

Loan cover is voluntary. A lender should let you choose the insurer, and take separate consent for it — not tie it to the loan.

RBI on loan cover and insurer choice
03

Details

Key points

A few questions settle most of the cost and the fine print.

  • Premium inside the loan If the premium is added to the loan, you pay interest on it for the remaining tenure. Ask for the premium as a separate figure, and whether you can pay it outside the loan.
  • Early repayment or transfer If you close the loan early or move it to another bank, this cover often cannot come with you. You may get only some of the premium back — ask for that amount in writing.
  • A term plan instead For many borrowers a plain term plan covers the loan for less and stays level as the loan falls. See loan cover for how the two compare.

Education only. Your Certificate of Insurance or Policy Schedule wins.
