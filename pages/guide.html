A loan secured
by your home.

The lender advances the amount. You repay monthly. Until closure, they hold a claim on the property papers.

Lender means a bank or housing finance company.

₹

How much you can borrow

Two limits apply. You receive roughly the lower one.

Property

The bank can lend only up to these percentages of the Agreement Value.

  • Agreement Value up to ₹30 lakh Bank can lend up to 90%.
  • Agreement Value ₹30 lakh to ₹75 lakh Bank can lend up to 80%.
  • Agreement Value above ₹75 lakh Bank can lend up to 75%.

These are the highest limits set by RBI.
Banks may approve a lower or higher amount depending on your profile and bank policy.

Income

The bank can lend only up to what your monthly EMI capacity supports.

  • Net take-home income per month
  • Less existing EMIs, and a share of your credit card limits
  • Whatever is left becomes your EMI capacity for the home loan.
  • From that EMI, rate, and tenure, the bank works out the loan amount.

All EMIs are usually limited to about 55–70% of take-home.
An earning co-applicant can help to increase the loan amount.

Equated Monthly Installment (EMI)

One monthly payment. Interest and principal.

What you pay

Each EMI splits into interest on the outstanding loan and principal that reduces the balance.

  • Early years: more of each EMI goes to interest
  • Later years: more of each EMI goes to principal

EMI stays the same while the rate stays the same. If the rate changes, EMI or tenure may change.

When it starts

Typically starts one month after first disbursement.

  • Ready home: full EMI after disbursement

Interest rate

Two types. Banks may offer either.

Floating Preferred

Moves with the benchmark over the loan tenure.

  • External benchmark (often the repo rate) + the bank’s spread
  • Reset at least once every 3 months as per RBI
  • If the rate rises, choose how the extra cost hits you: pay a lump sum, raise EMI, extend tenure, or mix these
  • Prepayment / foreclosure charges: None

Fixed / hybrid

Fixed for a set period, then the rate resets.

  • External benchmark (often the repo rate) + the bank’s spread + a fixed rate premium
  • Usually fixed for 3 or 5 years, as per the lender
  • Usually about 1% to 2% higher than floating during the fixed period
  • Prepayment / foreclosure charges: 2% to 4%

Switching fixed ↔ floating is available based on the lender’s policy, at applicable cost.

Before you commit

Ask the lender these before you sign.

  • Floating, or fixed for a set period then reset?
  • How long is the fixed period, and what happens after the rate resets?
  • Which external benchmark, and how often does it reset?
  • Can the bank’s spread change later?
  • What charges apply if you switch or prepay?
  • If the rate rises, what are your options?

Charges

Fees tied to getting the loan sanctioned and disbursed.

Bank fees

Fee the lender charges to process your case.

  • Processing fee A small amount taken upfront to log in your application.
  • Others Account handling fees or any other as per lender.

Other fees

Fees for checks and for registering the loan and security.

  • Legal and technical fees Fee to check legal papers and the property.
  • Title search report fees Fee to check the property title.
  • Valuation fees Fee to value the property.
  • Government charges Government fees to register the loan security.
